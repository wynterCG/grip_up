# M1: predictive hand capture

## Scope and preserved model

Implemented after M0 on `feature/v0.2-predictive-grip`. This is M1 of the v0.2
specification; it is not a completed v0.2. M2–M6 have not started. The baseline
tag/configuration/archive remain untouched, including the original Janga source.

The existing dynamic torso and axial tension arms remain authoritative. Shared
effort stays at 360 N / 150 Nm, hand travel at 4 m/s, anatomical reach at
0.65708 m, elastic physical cap at 0.66 m and catch blend at 100 ms. No force,
launch, collision, spring, mass or locomotion tuning was changed for M1.

The new predictor reads the current shoulder/hand state, COM linear/angular
velocity, support-local point and support contact-point velocity. A bounded
200 ms forecast (configuration clamped to 100–250 ms) estimates torso gravity
and existing arm effort, and a dynamic support's translation/rotation. It does
not simulate unknown future joint/contact impulses. Its results never authorize
attachment or write body pose, velocity or forces.

NOW means current request geometry is valid. SOON means a short future reach
window is predicted. Neither means an instantaneous or guaranteed catch. Contact
may lie beyond the forecast, in which case contact ETA and prediction error
remain -1. `predicted_target_time` identifies the time of the displayed estimate.

In hold mode a currently unreachable SOON point can begin a finite reaching
gesture if its predicted entry is within the 150 ms intent buffer. The selected
material-local point stays fixed; no automatic retarget/retry occurs. Before
entry the hand stays inside the current anatomical sphere and applies no force.
If entry does not occur in time, the gesture expires. Once it enters range,
normal reaching continues at the existing speed. Mouse-up, focus loss, settings
and reset cancel pending input. Existing attached hands are never replaced by
the buffer. Toggle mode keeps the previous immediate-request behavior.

The final evaluator uses CURRENT reach, endpoint proximity, a real surface
intersection and unobstructed shoulder access. It also records actual contact
speed and an energy/work diagnostic; this diagnostic does not inject assistance
or impose an arbitrary speed cutoff. Normal physical load/extension limits still
decide whether an attached catch can be held.

## Automated comparison

`python tools/run_regressions.py m1` produces the complete current report.
`docs/validation/m1/results.json` records **1,102 checks, zero failures**:
the original 962 checks plus 10 instrumentation, 18 real-input and 112 capture
matrix checks. All applicable suites run at both 60 and 120 Hz; the asset audit
runs once. Full console output is archived beside the JSON report.

At **each** frequency:

| Controlled cases | M1 OFF | M1 ON |
|---|---:|---:|
| Already reachable: stationary, crossed/offset, swing, upward, angular, moving support | 24/24 | 24/24 |
| Future-only: vertical translation, lateral swing, translating/rotating support | 0/12 accepted | 12/12 caught and retained |

Success requires real attachment and **one second of retention**, with neutral
input after contact. Four small pose variations per category are repeated.
These are deliberately favorable controlled conditions, not a claim of 100%
success on arbitrary gameplay targets or with continued contraction after a dyno.
The moving support uses the same 30 kg mass, `(3,3,3)` inertia, offset COM and
1.2 × 1 × 0.1 m geometry across favorable/adverse cases. Favorable trajectories
use lower relative velocity/spin; no core strength or support inertia was raised.

The matrix verifies disabled equivalence, finite anatomical hand paths, no force
before attachment, current-contact authorization, shared budgets, button release,
expiry, destroyed supports, obstruction, conveyor velocity semantics and COM
angular contact velocity. Prediction remains read-only, including hand metrics.

Maximum predicted entry-time error was 0 ms at 120 Hz and 8.33 ms at 60 Hz.
Maximum in-horizon contact-time error was 8.33 / 0 ms respectively. Only
the four forecasts per frequency that actually predicted in-horizon contact are counted in that
contact-error statistic; later arrivals are explicitly unknown.

## Rendered interaction

Godot MCP Pro drives `tests/capture_predictive.gd` in the real project under
`E:\Godot Games\grip_up`. It climbs six wall steps, charges/releases the normal
launch, then sends an actual held mouse-button event at the first suitable SOON
window after the original anchors release. It compares OFF/ON and saves the
rendered frames, arrival/retention report and sampled input/contact trace.

At 120 Hz, the target was approximately 0.763 m away before charging. The early
request arrived approximately 0.668–0.675 m from the shoulder, beyond its 0.65708 m
reach, with entry predicted in 8.33–16.67 ms. OFF rejected `out_of_reach`. ON accepted
the pending gesture, physically contacted the wall after about 200 ms and retained it
for a further second with normal W contraction. See `m1/rendered_120hz.json` and
the corresponding early/retained screenshots. The 60 Hz report is archived in
the same directory: OFF again rejects the early click; ON catches after 200 ms
and retains for a further second. Its request was approximately 0.712 m away,
with entry forecast in 41.67 ms. Trajectories are compared by outcome, not
identical pixels. The original three scenes were also replayed with flags OFF
during M0; those screenshots/traces remain in the baseline archive.

The native comparison demonstrates a functioning launch-followed-by-catch path.
It does not replace unfamiliar-player A/B ratings of fairness, control or feel.
Human evaluation remains pending; the experimental option defaults OFF.

## Failure boundaries and remaining work

Two adversarial matrix cases deliberately stay outside the favorable success
gate: a late descending angular launch and a faster unconstrained rotating
support. Both make legitimate contact but subsequently exceed physical arm
extension and release. An anticipated grip cannot make an unsafe trajectory
holdable. These outcomes remain in the full logs rather than being counted as
predictor successes. Future constrained support motion, unexpected collisions
and rapidly changing arm effort can invalidate a short-horizon estimate.

An earlier rendered probe released one original launch arm early and lost enough
momentum that the higher target never entered reach. That probe was corrected
to preserve the normal launch through its automatic release; it was not fixed
by adding force. A report-only loop condition initially failed to count a catch
that was already ATTACHED; the corrected helper records contact and retention.

The optional trace uses COM-based shoulder velocity. A position-error value is
reported only when a contact time was actually forecast; movement beyond the
forecast horizon is not mislabeled as prediction error. HUD failure reasons and
NOW/SOON/UNAVAILABLE colors remain advisory. Tab enables contact/normal/reach/force
lines, hidden during normal play. Capture JSONL logging remains opt-in.

The final speed audit includes the first ATTACHED frame, not just REACHING.
It exposed the previous 1 mm arrival tolerance adding a small endpoint snap.
M1 now waits for the finite step to reach the exact point (numerical epsilon),
and the predictor uses the same arrival rule. The baseline's original tolerance
is preserved with the feature OFF. Both frequency matrices pass this stronger
check with the same catch/retention outcomes.

Physical feet and leg push, camera-neutral support-aware posture, directional
grip quality, the new reactive challenge and mantle are future milestones.
In particular, the existing camera-referenced twist torque is unchanged and
must be addressed in M3. No human feel acceptance gate is claimed here.
