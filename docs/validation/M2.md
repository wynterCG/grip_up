# M2: independent physical foot support

## Scope

This milestone adds automatic physical foot contacts, a comparison scene and
instrumentation. It extends the M1 branch, preserved as `v0.2-M1` (`eeebaaa`).
The original `v0.1-baseline` remains unchanged. M3–M6 have not been implemented.

Enable **Controls → Automatic foot contacts**, then select **04 FOOT SUPPORT**.
Hold both hand targets, press W to extend the planted legs while pulling with
the arms, and use S to lower. The same checkbox compares identical geometry
with feet OFF/ON. No new limb-control buttons were added. Hand hold/release
and predictive capture remain independent. Feet start OFF in a fresh launch.

## Physical model and integration

Each foot has FREE, SEARCHING, PLANTING, SUPPORTING and SLIDING states. Its hip,
rest ankle and maximum length come from the biped's measured rest skeleton
(approximately 0.82979 m per leg). The visual skeleton consumes physical foot
targets; it never applies body transforms or changes bone lengths.

The local sampler checks current surfaces near each foot, a limited hip cone,
leg extension/flexion, hip-to-ankle visibility and the approach path. An available
raised, upward-bearing foothold scores better than a nearby wall with poor
vertical support. Search runs every 100 ms; retained contacts are sticky and
revalidated every physics step. No full climbing-route planner is present.

PLANTING has finite 2 m/s ankle motion, including its final contact frame.
Raised steps use a lift/over/plant path, with sweeps and anatomical checks at
each step. It applies zero force until the ankle reaches the current surface
with its measured sole clearance. Contact points and normals are support-local,
so translation and rotation retain the same material point. Missing, obstructed,
unreachable or disabled surfaces lose contact without a replacement impulse.

The leg is a compliant massless actuator. Its axial force is compression only,
with an implicit spring/damping denominator using actual relative contact-point
velocity and rotational effective mass. The result is projected into a unilateral
friction cone: N ≥ 0 and |T| ≤ μN. Effective friction is capped by both the foot
profile and an explicit support PhysicsMaterial when present. Excess tangential
demand produces SLIDING, moves the contact across real queried geometry and
releases it at an edge. An extended unloaded leg cannot pull on its support.

`resources/foot_tuning.gd` is the single source for leg tuning: K=1800, D=100,
25 mm initial compression preload, 120 ms engagement, 0.30 / 0.40 m/s extension /
lowering, μ=0.85, at most 120 N per foot and **180 N shared by both legs**.
The existing hand budget remains **360 N**. These are separate documented limb
effort budgets; the central controller combines their moments and the existing
assist into the same **150 Nm total climbing torque cap**. Adding a second foot
does not double the 180 N budget. Every scaled force remains inside its cone.

Both sides receive equal-and-opposite forces at the **same actual world contact
point**. Applying the torso side at that point represents the wrench transmitted
through the massless leg, including its moment; no hidden hip/contact couple is
discarded. Dynamic bodies receive real reactions, not scripted displacement.

Climbing intent permits new foot plants. Releasing all hands preserves existing
valid supporting feet, but does not acquire new ones or finish pending plants.
Landing the central sole hands control back to ground locomotion, which avoids
two competing walking/leg motors. If all actual contacts disappear, there is no
air propulsion. The existing arm launch temporarily disengages feet during its
charge/pull/cooldown; this milestone adds no separate leg launch boost.

## Verification

`python tools/run_regressions.py m2` records **1,294 passing checks**: all 1,102
M1 checks, 100 core foot checks, 34 integration checks and 58 additional original
route checks with physical feet ON across 60/120 Hz.
Complete outputs and `results.json` are in `docs/validation/m2/`.

Core fixtures cover flat and sloped support, a zero-friction wall, missing or
occluded footholds, excessive tangential demand, material-local moving contacts,
destroyed support bodies, disabled contacts, independent feet, finite movement,
no force before contact, compression-only behavior, reset, double-commit guards,
180 N magnitude allocation and actual dynamic force/momentum reactions.

A 12 kg body supported only by the legs settles near its 117.7 N gravity load.
W then raises it approximately **0.133 m**, with approximately **16.1 J** of
measured positive leg work, at both frequencies. Dynamic two-body reaction tests
measure linear momentum errors below 3×10⁻⁷ and zero angular error in the
controlled isotropic-inertia fixture; a separate eccentric-contact case also
checks actual angular response and conserved total momentum.

Full biped, identical scene/input comparison (one second W, settle, one second
load measurement; both hand grips retained):

| Measure | 120 Hz feet OFF → ON | 60 Hz feet OFF → ON |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean hand-force magnitude sum | 141.26 → 42.52 N | 137.80 → 43.39 N |
| Mean leg-force magnitude sum | 0 → 84.80 N | 0 → 84.11 N |
| Height gained over the trial | 0.191 → 0.284 m | 0.186 → 0.281 m |
| Positive leg work during W | 0 → 20.71 J | 0 → 20.95 J |

This shows roughly **69% less arm effort in this fixture**, with real positive
leg work. It does not claim a universal percentage improvement or extra airborne
reach. The integration suite also checks finite visual leg length, combined
torque allocation, independent foot stance after hand release, launch isolation,
feature toggling without dropping hands, reset and walking without a second
motor.

Godot MCP Pro runs `tests/capture_feet.gd` through the actual game window with
real held mouse events. Rendered OFF/ON reports, controls/debug screenshots and
20 Hz sampled JSONL are archived in `m2/` for both frequencies. Native timings
use real timers and screen ray intersections, so measurements differ from the
headless fixture; outcomes and load reduction, not identical trajectories, are
the comparison. Both native runs retain the two real hand grips and both
footholds; mean hand load falls from approximately 140/137 N to 23/23 N at
120/60 Hz, with approximately 104/103 N carried by the legs. Native rays aim at
the visible screen target, while the headless fixture samples the shoulder ray,
so their force totals are not treated as the same measurement.
No unfamiliar-player feel rating has been collected yet.

## Boundaries

Candidate search is local and heuristic; it may miss unusual overhangs or
complicated foothold approaches. It checks ankle/leg paths and sole clearance,
not a full articulated foot collision volume. The original torso/leg/sole body
proxies remain unchanged. An unobstructed foothold can still be unusable because
the body or knee configuration cannot reach it.

Planted feet can change torso roll and hand load; there is no new upright lock
or automatic balance solver. Existing camera-referenced twist assistance remains
until M3. Its limits and the absence of mantle/full ground transitions are not
fixed by this milestone. The feature remains opt-in pending human feel evaluation.
