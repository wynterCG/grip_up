# v0.6 early-target pilot

Use the same PC, route geometry,120Hz (then60Hz), arm policyB and P0 suspension.
Keep feet, Space, new posture refinement and old predictive/buffer comparisons
OFF. Only change **v0.6 select distant targets** in Controls. Startup remains
the earlier profile2; Controls/05 selects independent aim. The dedicated v0.6
entry buttons also opt into the experiment on the existing swing/beam scenes.

Hold LMB on guide00, then RMB on01. Once the second hand really says ATTACHED,
release the first button, aim at02 and hold it again. Repeat through10. With the
new option ON you may commit to a point while the hover marker is amber and the
hand still cannot reach. The outline marks your remembered material point;
TARGET PENDING is not a grip. Pointer/orbit cannot move that selection. Release
the relevant button to cancel; press again to choose a different point.

Compare three short reference runs with the flag OFF and three ON, alternating
order after a brief familiarisation. R restarts. Do not count losing both hands
and recovering as an uninterrupted completion. Record ten-transfer completion,
number of deliberately early selections, catch/transit/load failure reasons,
and whether waiting felt fair. Also try scene06: select the far reachable face
early, then deliberately pump/brake with existing WASD. A camera-visible point
can still have an obstructed shoulder approach. Scene03 tests moving material
points on the existing hinged beam.

After each run, Controls/Rate current session stores precision,comfort,weight,
fairness,retry,agency,recovery and readability on the existing1–5 scale. Include
failed runs. Report whether early selection reduced timing pressure while you
still chose how to move and transfer load. A long held target is an intention,
not a promise that the selected point will ever become physically accessible.

Attempt/event JSONL and per-session summaries are under
`user://transfer_sessions/`. Optional sampled traces separately expose actual
shoulder/endpoint/goal, material point/normal, reach margin, obstruction, held
time, world/transport/gesture speeds, actual contact and acquisition load.
The 4m/s muscle gesture is relative to the moving shoulder frame; the body's
own translation/rotation can carry its hand faster in world space. A rare
impossible swept-workspace conflict explicitly cancels, rather than fabricating
a valid contact. Inactive invalid historical endpoints use a passive visual
fallback until finite FREE recovery becomes anatomically valid.

No automated route, scripted native demonstration or selection count substitutes
for this comparison. Human improvement remains pending until feedback arrives.
